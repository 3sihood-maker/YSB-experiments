Paul Challenge

Connect a young athlete's real training schedule — the one they already follow — with an online challenge to be active over a longer stretch, perhaps a month.

The shape of it

You set your training schedule up front. Over the period you read about Paul's missionary journeys, interact with the Bible as you go, and track your activity against the plan you set. The three run together rather than sitting side by side: the reading advances because the training does.

The goal is a medal — and the medal does not just appear and vanish. It lands in your Achievements, so what you finish stays somewhere you can go back and look at.

Pairing a journey with a sport

Paul's journeys are already distances — long ones, over land and sea. Each one pairs with a sport: pentathlon, swimming, running, sailing, race walking. The challenge is measured in whatever unit that sport actually uses, so it reads like training rather than like homework.

The pairing is what makes a testimony land alongside it. We already have a swimmer's testimony, so that is the obvious one to build first.

Worked example

Paul's final journey to Rome paired with a swimmer. The journey runs Jerusalem → Caesarea → Sidon → Myra → Fair Havens → Malta → Syracuse → Rhegium → Puteoli → Rome, and a month of pool sessions accumulates against it. The letters Paul wrote at this time — Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians and Philemon — are what you read and interact with as you go.

Effort, then reading, then the medal

The journey breaks into sections that map onto the weeks of the schedule. Each section takes both things — the training done, and the Bible interaction — and finishing one earns something.

Two ways to handle the reward, and the second is the more interesting one. Either each section hands over a badge outright, or each section reveals another part of one medal — the ship, the shore, the snake, the laurel, the words around the rim — so the whole thing only resolves when the month does. Either way it ends up on the Achievements shelf.

Still open. Which sport goes with which of the other three journeys; whether the distance is the real thing or scaled to what a young athlete's actual schedule covers in a month; whether activity is entered by hand or read from something they already use; and whether the medal assembles per journey or once across the whole set.
