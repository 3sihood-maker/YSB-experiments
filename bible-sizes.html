Bible Sizes

How long is a cubit?

A cubit is the length of a forearm, elbow to fingertip. It was never standardised the way a metre is, so every figure here shifts depending on which one you choose.

17.5 inches · 44.5 cm

The short or common cubit, at the lower end of the range suggested by excavated Israelite buildings and water systems. Some measurements at Hezekiah's tunnel and Iron Age structures sit near this.

18 inches · 45.7 cm

The convention most English Bibles and study notes use. It is a round working figure rather than an archaeological finding, which is why it turns up so often in margins and footnotes.

20.6 inches · 52.3 cm

The royal or long cubit, known from Egypt and Mesopotamia, where a "cubit and a handbreadth" made a longer official measure. Ezekiel 40:5 and 43:13 describe exactly that longer cubit, so it is the right one for the temple visions.

Why it matters

Across this range Noah's Ark varies by about 24 metres end to end, and Goliath by half a metre. Showing the spread is more honest than picking one and calling it the answer.

Distances are route estimates over terrain, not straight lines, and are rounded. The Temple Mount platform is measured archaeologically rather than given in cubits, so the cubit buttons do not apply to it.
